Political Philosophy: Advanced Topics

Track Index

Learners are encouraged to select one or two tracks aligned with their specific normative, institutional, or regional interests rather than attempting to study all five tracks sequentially.

  • Democratic Theory - Advanced study of democratic legitimacy, deliberative decision-making, epistemic proceduralism, and institutional innovations such as citizen assemblies and sortition.
  • Global Justice and Human Rights - Normative inquiries into transnational redistribution, territorial rights, the philosophical foundations of human rights, and global climate obligations.
  • Identity, Multiculturalism, and Feminist Political Philosophy - Critical investigations into group-differentiated citizenship, structural oppression, epistemic injustice, and feminist re-evaluations of the state and the family.
  • Advanced Distributive Justice and Egalitarianism - Post-Rawlsian debates regarding the proper metric of equality, comparing luck egalitarianism, relational equality, the capabilities approach, and the moral limits of markets.
  • Comparative and Non-Western Political Philosophy - Systematic explorations of political authority, popular sovereignty, and civic order within modern Islamic, Confucian, and African traditions.

Democratic Theory

Advanced examination of democratic legitimacy, evaluating deliberative decision-making, collective intelligence, and participatory institutional design beyond conventional majoritarian voting.

Deliberative Democracy: Deliberative Democracy: Essays on Reason and Politics (James Bohman & William Rehg, eds. / MIT Press) - The canonical anthology compiling foundational papers by Jürgen Habermas, Joshua Cohen, John Rawls, and David Estlund that establish the deliberative model of collective decision-making.

Epistemic Democracy: Democratic Authority: A Philosophical Framework (David M. Estlund / Princeton University Press) - A rigorous defense of epistemic proceduralism, arguing that democracy derives legitimacy in part from its ability to track correct outcomes without collapsing into authoritarian rule by experts.

Democratic Innovations and Mini-Publics: Videos and Podcast (Stanford Deliberative Democracy Lab) - A free media series featuring James Fishkin and collaborators that bridges normative theory and institutional design through deliberative polling, sortition, and practical citizen assemblies.

Institutional Innovation Design: Democratic Innovations: Designing Institutions for Citizen Participation (Graham Smith / Cambridge University Press) - An authoritative comparative analysis assessing how real-world institutional mechanisms, such as participatory budgeting and citizens' assemblies, realize core democratic goods.

Global Justice and Human Rights

The extension of normative political philosophy beyond domestic borders, investigating international redistribution, territorial rights, the foundation of human rights, and global climate duties.

Foundations of Global Justice: Political Philosophy: Global Justice, 17.000J / 24.611J (MIT OpenCourseWare / Joshua Cohen, Thomas Scanlon, Amartya Sen) - A comprehensive graduate-level seminar examining cosmopolitanism, state sovereignty, international distributive justice, and border control.

Global Poverty and Institutional Harms: World Poverty and Human Rights (Thomas Pogge / Polity Press) - The defining cosmopolitan treatise arguing that affluent countries violate negative duties of justice by upholding an international institutional order that foreseeably produces severe deprivation.

The Philosophy of Human Rights: The Idea of Human Rights (Charles R. Beitz / Oxford University Press) - A leading formulation of the political conception of human rights, treating them as international discursive standards that limit domestic sovereignty and justify external interference.

Environmental and Climate Justice: Environmental Justice (University of Michigan / Coursera) - A free-to-audit course exploring procedural, distributive, and restorative justice across global environmental crises, indigenous land claims, and climate inequities.

Environmental and Climate Justice: A Perfect Moral Storm: The Ethical Tragedy of Climate Change (Stephen M. Gardiner / Oxford University Press) - The seminal philosophical monograph detailing how the convergence of spatial dispersion, intergenerational fragmentation, and institutional inadequacy obstructs climate justice.

Identity, Multiculturalism, and Feminist Political Philosophy

Critical examination of group-differentiated citizenship, structural oppression, epistemic marginalization, and feminist re-evaluations of the public-private divide.

Multiculturalism and Minority Rights: Multicultural Citizenship: A Liberal Theory of Minority Rights (Will Kymlicka / Oxford University Press) - The seminal work demonstrating that group-differentiated rights for national minorities and polyethnic groups can be defended within a liberal framework of individual autonomy.

Feminist Political Theory: Feminist Thought, WGS.301J (MIT OpenCourseWare / Sally Haslanger) - A structured OpenCourseWare seminar examining ideological constructions of gender, the public-private divide, the gendered division of labor, and the political dimensions of social power.

Structural Injustice and Oppression: Justice and the Politics of Difference (Iris Marion Young / Princeton University Press) - A foundational critique of the distributive paradigm of justice, arguing that political philosophy must focus on structural oppression, domination, and institutional decision-making structures.

Epistemic Injustice: Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing (Miranda Fricker / Oxford University Press) - The definitive monograph establishing the concepts of testimonial and hermeneutical injustice, illustrating how social prejudices undermine marginalized persons in their capacities as knowers.

Advanced Distributive Justice and Egalitarianism

Post-Rawlsian inquiries into the proper metric of egalitarian concern, comparing luck egalitarianism, relational and democratic equality, capabilities, and the commodification of civic goods.

Luck Egalitarianism: Rescuing Justice and Equality (G. A. Cohen / Harvard University Press) - A demanding analytical critique of Rawls's difference principle, presenting an influential case that justice requires eliminating unchosen disadvantages and applying an egalitarian ethos to personal choice.

Relational Equality: What Is the Point of Equality? (Elizabeth S. Anderson / Ethics) - A freely accessible, landmark paper that critiques luck egalitarian preoccupation with cosmic bad luck, arguing instead that egalitarianism's true aim is eliminating social hierarchy and oppression.

The Capabilities Approach: Creating Capabilities: The Human Development Approach (Martha C. Nussbaum / Harvard University Press) - A concise philosophical presentation of the capabilities framework, articulating the central constitutional entitlements required for a life worthy of human dignity.

The Moral Limits of Markets: Why Some Things Should Not Be for Sale: The Moral Limits of Markets (Debra Satz / Oxford University Press) - A systematic philosophical inquiry into "noxious markets," analyzing how commodifying certain goods undermines agency, exploits asymmetric vulnerability, and erodes the conditions for democratic citizenship.

Comparative and Non-Western Political Philosophy

Systematic inquiry into political authority, legitimacy, and collective flourishing across Islamic, Confucian, and African philosophical traditions.

Modern Islamic Political Thought: The Caliphate of Man: Popular Sovereignty in Modern Islamic Thought (Andrew F. March / Harvard University Press) - A comprehensive philosophical analysis of how twentieth-century Islamic theorists reconciled divine sovereignty with democratic self-governance through the doctrine of universal human vicegerency.

Confucian Political Meritocracy: The China Model: Political Meritocracy and the Limits of Democracy (Daniel A. Bell / Princeton University Press) - A comparative treatise evaluating the historical and contemporary viability of political meritocracy, civil service examinations, and multi-tier governance as alternatives to Western electoral democracy.

African Communitarianism and Legitimacy: Tradition and Modernity: Philosophical Reflections on the African Experience (Kwame Gyekye / Oxford University Press) - A foundational monograph on African political philosophy exploring moderate communitarianism, democratic legitimacy, and the philosophical reconciliation of indigenous political values with the modern postcolonial state.

African Political Thought and Anticolonial Liberation: African Political Thought (Stephen Chan / SOAS University of London) - A ten-lecture university video series surveying anticolonial liberation, Negritude, African socialism, and the intellectual history of modern African governance; search "African Political Thought Stephen Chan SOAS" on YouTube.
